# Prespecified study protocol

## Research question

**When do synthetic health datasets preserve the answer to a causal research question, and when can conventional statistical fidelity be misleading about their usefulness for causal inference?**

## Estimand

The primary estimand is the average treatment effect (ATE):

\[
\tau = E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}.
\]

The expectation is over the semi-synthetic reference population generated by sampling baseline covariates from the cleaned BRFSS pool.

## Identification structure

The causal graph is deliberately minimal: baseline covariates `X` affect treatment `D` and outcome `Y`, and `D` affects `Y`. There are no mediators, instruments, or post-treatment covariates in the MVP.

Identification of the observational ATE is based on consistency, conditional exchangeability given X, and positivity. Because this is a simulation, those assumptions are encoded by construction and their practical consequences can be stress-tested.

## DGP

BRFSS provides a realistic empirical distribution for X. Treatment is generated as

\[
D_i \sim Bernoulli(e(X_i)),
\]

with a nonlinear score containing age, BMI, income, general health, diabetes, physical activity, sex, an age×diabetes interaction, and a quadratic BMI term. The intercept is calibrated in every replicate to target 45% treatment prevalence.

The untreated potential outcome is

\[
Y_i(0) = m(X_i) + \epsilon_i,
\]

where `m(X)` is nonlinear and includes age, BMI, income, activity, diabetes, education, general health, a quadratic BMI term, and an age×health interaction. The primary treatment effect is constant:

\[
Y_i(1)=Y_i(0)+2.
\]

The shared error term means the individual treatment effect is exactly known. The outcome is a **simulated health-risk score in arbitrary units**, not a measured clinical endpoint.

### Variable roles

- Confounders: age, BMI, income, physical activity, diabetes, general health.
- Treatment-only predictor: sex.
- Outcome-only predictor: education.
- Effect modifiers: none in the primary analysis; a heterogeneous-effect option exists for later robustness work.

## Positivity scenarios

1. **Standard overlap** — propensity-score scale = 1.
2. **Weak overlap** — propensity-score scale = 2, creating substantially more observations near treatment probabilities 0 and 1.

Only these two scenarios are primary. The project intentionally avoids a large scenario grid.

## Synthetic-data generators

1. **Empirical Gaussian copula** — transparent baseline with empirical continuous marginals and latent-normal categorical intervals.
2. **CTGAN** — optional deep generative benchmark for mixed tabular data.

The full protocol compares both. CTGAN is an optional dependency because it is computationally heavier; the core repository and CI remain usable without it.

## Conventional utility

- continuous marginal KS distance;
- standardized mean difference;
- categorical total variation distance;
- correlation-matrix distortion;
- real-vs-synthetic classifier AUC and raw pMSE;
- descriptive train-on-synthetic/test-on-reference treatment AUC and outcome RMSE.

A simple `descriptive_fidelity_distance` averages selected marginal distances for plotting only. It is **not** presented as a causal-utility index.

## Causal estimators

A fixed hierarchy is applied identically to reference and synthetic data:

1. crude difference in means — deliberately confounded baseline;
2. cross-fitted g-computation with histogram gradient boosting;
3. Hájek-normalized IPW using a compact logistic propensity model;
4. cross-fitted AIPW with flexible nuisance functions — primary estimator.

DML, TMLE, causal forests, and CATE estimation are deferred because they do not answer the primary question more directly.

## Causal-utility metrics

At replicate level:

- ATE error and absolute error relative to known truth;
- relative absolute error;
- reference-estimate distortion;
- confidence-interval coverage where an inferential estimator supplies a CI;
- sign preservation.

Across Monte Carlo replicates:

- bias;
- mean absolute error;
- RMSE;
- empirical SD;
- mean estimated SE;
- SE ratio = mean estimated SE / empirical SD;
- 95% coverage;
- mean CI width;
- sign preservation.

Mechanism diagnostics also evaluate whether models learned from synthetic data reproduce the known treatment propensity and treatment-effect contrast on the original X grid.

## Monte Carlo sizes

- `smoke.yaml`: execution/testing only.
- `pilot.yaml`: small computational validation; **not a basis for scientific conclusions**.
- `full.yaml`: n=4,000, 100 replications, two scenarios, Gaussian copula + CTGAN. This is the intended main study.

The full replication count is chosen to make coverage and RMSE substantially more interpretable than the small pilot, subject to a final compute-precision check.

## Privacy scope

This project evaluates **utility after synthesis**. It does not establish differential privacy, anonymity, resistance to membership inference, or any formal disclosure-risk guarantee. Synthetic data are never described as automatically private.
