# Prespecified study protocol

## Research question

**When do synthetic health datasets preserve the answer to a causal research question, and when can conventional statistical fidelity be misleading about their usefulness for causal inference?**

## Estimand

The primary estimand is the average treatment effect (ATE):

$$
\tau_{ATE}=\mathbb E\!\left[Y(1)-Y(0)\right].
$$

In the primary data-generating process,

$$
Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)=2\quad\forall i,
$$

so that

$$
\tau_{ATE}=2.
$$

The expectation is over the semi-synthetic reference population generated by sampling baseline covariates from the cleaned BRFSS pool.

## Identification structure

The causal graph is deliberately minimal: baseline covariates $X$ affect treatment $D$ and outcome $Y$, and $D$ affects $Y$. There are no mediators, instruments, or post-treatment covariates in the primary design.

The observational ATE is identified under consistency,

$$
Y_i=Y_i(D_i),
$$

conditional exchangeability,

$$
Y_i(d)\perp D_i\mid X_i,
\qquad d\in\{0,1\},
$$

and positivity,

$$
0<\Pr(D_i=1\mid X_i)<1.
$$

Because the treatment and potential outcomes are simulated, these conditions are encoded by construction. The weak-overlap scenario is used to stress-test practical positivity rather than to impose deterministic treatment.

## Mathematical data-generating process

BRFSS supplies only the empirical distribution of the baseline covariates $X$. Treatment and potential outcomes are generated from the structural equations below.

### Baseline notation

For individual $i$, let

- $A_i$ denote age, represented by the midpoint of the BRFSS age group;
- $B_i$ denote BMI;
- $I_i$ denote income category;
- $G_i$ denote general-health category;
- $E_i$ denote education category;
- $F_i$ denote the female indicator;
- $P_i$ denote the physical-activity indicator;
- $C_i$ denote the diabetes indicator.

For $V\in\{A,B,I,G,E\}$, define the within-replicate standardization

$$
Z_{V,i}=\frac{V_i-\bar V}{s_V},
\qquad
s_V^2=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(V_i-\bar V)^2.
$$

This is the population-standard-deviation convention used by the code in every Monte Carlo replicate.

### Treatment model

Define the nonlinear treatment score

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_i ={}&
0.35Z_{A,i}
+0.25Z_{B,i}
-0.30Z_{I,i}
+0.35Z_{G,i}
+0.45C_i
-0.25P_i
+0.10F_i\\
&+0.18Z_{A,i}C_i
+0.12\left(Z_{B,i}^2-1\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

The propensity score is

$$
e_i
\equiv
\Pr(D_i=1\mid X_i)
=
\Lambda\!\left(\alpha_s+\kappa_s r_i\right),
$$

where

$$
\Lambda(u)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-u}}
$$

and the scenario-specific overlap scale is

$$
\kappa_s=
\begin{cases}
1, & s=\text{standard},\\
2, & s=\text{weak overlap}.
\end{cases}
$$

The intercept $\alpha_s$ is recalibrated in every replicate so that mean treatment probability equals 0.45:

$$
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\Lambda\!\left(\alpha_s+\kappa_s r_i\right)
=0.45.
$$

Treatment is sampled as

$$
D_i\mid X_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(e_i).
$$

Thus the weak-overlap scenario changes only the scale of the treatment score; it leaves both the outcome model and the causal effect unchanged.

### Potential-outcome model

The untreated conditional outcome mean is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{0i} ={}&
50
+2.8Z_{G,i}
+1.5Z_{B,i}
+1.1Z_{A,i}
-1.0Z_{I,i}
-1.2P_i
+2.0C_i\\
&-0.6Z_{E,i}
+0.8\left(Z_{B,i}^2-1\right)
+0.7Z_{A,i}Z_{G,i}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The disturbance is generated as

$$
\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,5^2).
$$

The primary treatment effect is constant:

$$
\tau_i=2.
$$

Potential outcomes are therefore

$$
Y_i(0)=\mu_{0i}+\varepsilon_i,
$$

$$
Y_i(1)=\mu_{0i}+2+\varepsilon_i.
$$

Observed outcome is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y_i
&=(1-D_i)Y_i(0)+D_iY_i(1)\\
&=\mu_{0i}+2D_i+\varepsilon_i.
\end{aligned}
$$

Because the same disturbance enters both potential outcomes,

$$
Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)=2
$$

for every observation, and the true ATE is exactly 2.

The outcome is a **simulated health-risk score in arbitrary units**, not a measured clinical endpoint.

### Variable roles

- **Confounders:** age, BMI, income, physical activity, diabetes, general health.
- **Treatment-only predictor:** sex.
- **Outcome-only predictor:** education.
- **Effect modifiers:** none in the primary analysis; a heterogeneous-effect option exists for later robustness work but is not part of the reported primary experiment.

### Leakage boundary

The truth object contains

$$
\left\{e_i,Y_i(0),Y_i(1),\tau_i\right\},
$$

but the analysis/synthesis frame contains only

$$
W_i=(X_i,D_i,Y_i).
$$

The truth variables are removed before synthesis or estimation.

## Positivity scenarios

The two primary scenarios differ only through $\kappa_s$ in the propensity model:

1. **Standard overlap:** $\kappa_s=1$.
2. **Weak overlap:** $\kappa_s=2$.

The larger scale pushes more propensity scores toward 0 and 1 while holding the mean treatment probability near 0.45.

## Mathematical Gaussian-copula synthesis model

The primary generator is an empirical Gaussian copula fitted to

```math
W_i=(X_i,D_i,Y_i).
```

For each continuous variable $W_j$, let $R_{ij}$ denote its average rank. The empirical probability and latent-normal score are

```math
U_{ij}=\frac{R_{ij}-0.5}{n},
\qquad
Z_{ij}=\Phi^{-1}(U_{ij}).
```

For a categorical variable with categories $c=1,\ldots,K$ and empirical probabilities $p_c$, define the cumulative lower bound by

```math
L_c=\sum_{h=1}^{c-1}p_h.
```

The observed category is represented by the midpoint of its probability interval on the latent-normal scale:

```math
Z(c)=\Phi^{-1}\left(L_c+\frac{p_c}{2}\right).
```

Let $Z_i$ denote the resulting latent vector. Its empirical correlation matrix is projected to a valid correlation matrix $\widehat{R}$. Synthetic latent rows are sampled from

```math
Z_i^{\ast}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\widehat{R}\right).
```

They are then mapped to the unit interval by

```math
U_i^{\ast}=\Phi\left(Z_i^{\ast}\right).
```

Continuous variables are reconstructed using empirical quantile interpolation. Categorical variables are reconstructed by assigning $U_{ij}^{\ast}$ to the category whose empirical cumulative-probability interval contains it.

This generator is used as a transparent dependence-preserving baseline, not as a claim of state-of-the-art synthesis.

CTGAN remains an optional deep-generative extension. Its integration is tested separately, but no CTGAN scientific result is part of the reported primary experiment.

## Conventional utility

The study evaluates:

- continuous marginal KS distance;
- standardized mean difference;
- categorical total variation distance;
- correlation-matrix distortion;
- real-vs-synthetic classifier AUC and raw pMSE;
- descriptive train-on-synthetic/test-on-reference treatment AUC and outcome RMSE.

A simple `descriptive_fidelity_distance` averages selected marginal distances for plotting only. It is **not** presented as a causal-utility index.

## Causal estimators

A fixed hierarchy is applied identically to reference and synthetic data:

1. crude difference in means — deliberately confounded baseline;
2. cross-fitted g-computation with histogram gradient boosting;
3. Hájek-normalized IPW using a compact main-effects logistic propensity model;
4. cross-fitted AIPW with flexible nuisance functions — primary estimator.

The treatment DGP contains nonlinear terms and an interaction that the compact IPW propensity model does not include. IPW is therefore intentionally a **misspecification-sensitive benchmark**, not the correctly specified primary estimator. This distinction is important when separating estimator error on the reference data from additional distortion induced by synthesis.

DML, TMLE, causal forests, and CATE estimation are deferred because they do not answer the primary question more directly.

## Causal-utility metrics

At replicate level:

- ATE error and absolute error relative to known truth;
- relative absolute error;
- synthetic-minus-reference estimate distortion for the same scenario, replicate, and estimator;
- confidence-interval coverage where an inferential estimator supplies a CI;
- sign preservation.

Across Monte Carlo replicates:

- bias;
- mean absolute error;
- RMSE relative to known truth;
- mean synthetic-minus-reference estimate distortion;
- mean absolute synthetic-reference distortion;
- RMSE of synthetic-reference distortion;
- correlation between synthetic and corresponding reference estimates;
- empirical SD;
- mean estimated SE;
- SE ratio = mean estimated SE / empirical SD;
- 95% coverage;
- mean CI width;
- sign preservation.

The within-dataset SEs are analyst-facing model-based uncertainty estimates. For synthetic datasets they do **not** incorporate uncertainty from estimating the synthesis model or from the original confidential/reference sample. Their adequacy is assessed empirically through Monte Carlo SD, SE/SD ratios, and coverage rather than assumed.

Mechanism diagnostics also evaluate whether models learned from synthetic data reproduce the known treatment propensity and fitted treatment-effect contrast on the original $X$ grid. Because the primary DGP has a constant individual treatment effect, correlations between fitted treatment-contrast vectors are secondary diagnostics: any fitted heterogeneity reflects estimation noise or model approximation rather than true effect modification. For the primary question, ATE preservation and contrast RMSE are more interpretable than contrast correlation.

## Monte Carlo sizes

- `smoke.yaml`: execution/testing only.
- `pilot.yaml`: small computational validation; **not a basis for scientific conclusions**.
- `main_gaussian.yaml`: $n=4{,}000$, 100 replications, two scenarios, Gaussian copula only. This is the primary application study.
- `full.yaml`: broader Gaussian copula + CTGAN configuration retained for optional later benchmarking; it is not claimed as completed unless those runs exist.

With 100 replications, a nominal 95% coverage estimate has Monte Carlo standard error of about 2.2 percentage points. Coverage should therefore be interpreted with that simulation uncertainty in mind.

## BRFSS preprocessing scope

The empirical covariate pool is complete-case after restricting to the eight DGP covariates. BRFSS special missing codes are screened before recoding. BMI outside 12–60 kg/m² is screened as a **study-specific plausibility choice**, not as an official BRFSS validity rule; this choice should be reported and can be revisited in sensitivity analysis.

Survey weights are not used because the target is the empirical covariate distribution used by the simulation, not a population-representative U.S. adult estimand.

## Privacy scope

This project evaluates **utility after synthesis**. It does not establish differential privacy, anonymity, resistance to membership inference, or any formal disclosure-risk guarantee. Synthetic data are never described as automatically private.
